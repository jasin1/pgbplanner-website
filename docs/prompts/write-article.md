# Write a blog article from an approved brief

## Your role

You write the draft. You do not decide what the article is about.

Every decision about keyword, promise, scope, facts and CTA has already been
made and is recorded in the brief. Your job is to turn those decisions into a
finished Dutch article.

## Input

Fill in before running:

- Brief: docs/marketing/briefs/artikel-NN-slug.md

Also read, in this order:
- docs/marketing/editorial-guide.md — tone, principles, hard style rules
- docs/marketing/lexicon.md — word choices
- docs/marketing/blog-product-cta-spec.md — CTA form and placement

If any of these contradict each other, the editorial guide wins on
principles, the brief wins on this specific article.

## What you may do

Write freely at the craft level: structure, headings, transitions,
explanation, worked examples, paragraph order, sentence rhythm. The brief
sets the decisions, not the prose.

## What you may not do

Do not introduce factual claims, topics, scope, conclusions or product
promises that do not follow from the approved brief.

Concretely:
- Factual figures — rules, limits, statutory amounts, percentages, dates —
  must come from the brief's claim table.
- Illustrative figures inside a worked example do not need to be in the
  claim table, provided they are clearly presented as an example, are
  internally consistent, and suggest no new factual claim.
- No research. Do not search the web, do not consult other sources.
- Nothing listed under "Buiten scope" in the brief.
- No CTA copy of your own. Copy the heading, body and button text from the
  brief verbatim.

If something is missing that you need to write the article, stop and report
what is missing. Do not fill the gap yourself.

## How to handle claims

For each claim in the brief's claim table:
- Match your phrasing to its status. The lexicon defines which phrasing goes
  with which status.
- Keep every nuance or limitation from the nuance column visible in the
  article. A nuance that disappears is a factual error.

Sources go in footnotes or a source list at the end of the article, never as
interruptions in the running text.

## Structure

Open with the haakzin from the brief. Reproduce its situation and feeling;
the exact wording may be adapted to flow.

Then follow the content model from the editorial guide: search question,
underlying worry, practical meaning, forward look, product.

Place the CTA at the content moment the brief specifies, not at a fixed
position or word count.

## Output

- One article file, in the same location and format as existing articles.
- Slug exactly as given in the brief.
- Frontmatter matching existing articles.
- Title from the brief. Write a meta description derived from the brief's
  promise; it may not promise more than the promise does.
- Internal links as listed in the brief.
- Dutch. All of the following are Dutch and must not be translated: the
  article text, the haakzin, the CTA copy, the title, the meta description.

Do not commit. Report what you wrote and where.

## Final pass

Before reporting, read the finished draft from start to finish as one
continuous piece. Repair anything that reads as a separate block rather
than part of one article. Report that this was done.

This is not permission to introduce new factual content, new claims, or
wider scope. Fix flow and transitions only.

## Hard style rules

These come from the editorial guide and are the ones most often broken:

- No em-dashes anywhere in the published article text.
- No step-by-step framing. The reader manages their own budget and often has
  for years.
- No empty hedging. Instead of "dit geldt niet in iedere situatie", state
  precisely for whom it does and does not apply.
